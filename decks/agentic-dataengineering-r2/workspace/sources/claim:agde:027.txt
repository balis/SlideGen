FULL EXCERPT (from search result content):
"PSI = Σ(Ci - Ri) × ln(Ci/Ri), where Ci represents the proportion in the current distribution
and Ri represents the proportion in the reference distribution."

"Less than 0.1: insignificant change, no action required."
"0.1–0.25: some minor change, check other scorecard monitoring metrics."
"Greater than 0.25: major shift in population, need to delve deeper."

"PSI is a statistical tool used to measure how much the distribution of data changes between two
datasets, usually between a training dataset and a new dataset."

"PSI is widely used, especially in credit risk modeling, to measure the change in the distribution
of a variable between two populations (reference vs. current). It's applicable to both numerical
(after binning) and categorical features."

"The population data is often divided into bins or ranges based on specific characteristics.
The differences in each bin between the expected distribution and the target distribution are
utilized to calculate PSI."

NOTE: The original threshold commonly cited is PSI < 0.1 (no change), 0.1–0.2 (moderate), > 0.2
(significant). The 0.25 upper boundary also appears in some industry sources. Both sets of thresholds
are rule-of-thumb conventions from credit scoring, not formally derived cutoffs.
