Title: Data Distribution Shifts and Monitoring
Author: Chip Huyen
Retrieved: 2026-06-15

FULL EXCERPT:
The formal definitions of distribution shift types are grounded in the decomposition of the joint
distribution P(X, Y) into P(Y|X)·P(X) or P(X|Y)·P(Y).

"Covariate shift: when P(X) changes, but P(Y|X) remains the same."

"Concept drift: when P(Y|X) changes, but P(X) remains the same."

Example given for concept drift: "a 3 bedroom apartment in San Francisco could cost $2,000,000.
However, at the beginning of COVID-19...the same house would cost only $1,500,000." The price model
P(price | features) has changed even though the features P(X) themselves have not.

Schema drift (structural change such as a column being added, removed, or retyped) is categorised
separately from statistical distributional shift. Schema changes invalidate pipelines at parse time,
whereas distribution shifts pass structural validation silently.

SUPPORTING CONTEXT FROM SEARCH (corroborating definitions):
Search result summary: "Data drift refers to the changing distribution of the data to which the model
is applied. Data drift occurs when the distribution of input data changes over time while the underlying
relationship between inputs and outputs remains the same. Concept drift refers to a changing underlying
goal or objective for the model — when the relationship between input features and the target variable
changes. Schema drift is the unexpected evolution of data structure (e.g., column changes)."
Source: Dataversity, https://www.dataversity.net/articles/data-drift-vs-concept-drift-what-is-the-difference/
