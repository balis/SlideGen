FULL EXCERPT (from search result content):
"KL divergence is a non-symmetric measure of the difference between two distributions:
if you swap the baseline distribution p(x) and sample distribution q(x), you will get a
different number."

"PSI is a symmetric derivation of KL Divergence, mathematically equivalent to
KL(P||Q) + KL(Q||P)."

"KL Divergence and PSI are the two metrics which are commonly used to monitor data drift
in model monitoring."

KL DIVERGENCE FORMULA (from separate search result):
"D(P || Q) = Σ P(x) log[P(x)/Q(x)]
where P and Q are the two probability distributions being compared, and x is a particular
value that the distributions can take on."

ADDITIONAL CONTEXT ON PRODUCTION USE:
"In model monitoring, we almost exclusively use the discrete form of KL divergence and obtain
the discrete distributions by binning data."
"KL divergence is typically applied to each feature independently; it is not designed as a
covariant feature measure but rather a metric that shows how each feature has diverged
independently from the baseline values."
"The most common baselines are either a trailing window of production data or the training datasets."
Source: Arize AI blog on KL Divergence (https://arize.com/blog-course/kl-divergence/)

ASYMMETRY NOTE:
"There are times when teams want to swap comparison baselines, and having a metric where A/B
differs from B/A can make comparing results difficult. This is why tools like Arize often use
PSI, a symmetric variant of KL divergence, for monitoring distribution changes."
